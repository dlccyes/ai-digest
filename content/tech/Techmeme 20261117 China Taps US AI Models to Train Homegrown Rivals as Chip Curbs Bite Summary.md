[Techmeme surfaced](https://www.techmeme.com/261117/h0230) Reuters' November 17 investigation, [“China taps US AI models to train homegrown rivals as chip curbs bite.”](https://www.reuters.com/world/china/china-taps-us-ai-models-train-homegrown-rivals-chip-curbs-bite-2026-11-17/) After reviewing more than 300 Chinese academic papers, patents, company disclosures, recruitment notices, and government documents, Reuters found that model distillation has moved from isolated experiments into an industrial practice. The story is not that China has found a way to copy an American frontier model wholesale. It is that useful pieces of a costly model's behavior can be turned into reusable training data, making smaller domestic systems cheaper to build and deploy under tightening chip constraints.

## One expensive answer can become many cheap lessons

Distillation uses a strong “teacher” model to generate answers, plans, code, or demonstrations that train a smaller “student” model. The student does not inherit the teacher's complete capability. It learns selected behaviors from examples, often for a narrower task. That distinction matters: distillation reduces the cost of reproducing useful skills, but it does not remove the need for original research, high-quality data, engineering, or substantial computing power.

The documents give the process concrete form. In a November 2024 paper, researchers at Shanghai Jiao Tong University selected about 5,000 answers from OpenAI's o1, translated them with GPT-4o mini, and assembled 10,750 bilingual examples to further train Alibaba's Qwen2.5-72B. An April 2025 Alibaba paper described using GPT-4 and GPT-4o to generate multilingual material, then refining and scoring the responses before training smaller Qwen models. In February 2026, researchers from Shanghai Jiao Tong University and Meituan used GPT-4o to produce plans for coordinating coding agents, helping a smaller model learn how to organize multi-agent work.

These are more informative than a generic accusation of copying. They show a production pipeline: ask a capable model hard questions, retain the useful outputs, clean and score them, and train a system that can reproduce part of that performance without consulting the original provider on every request. A former Alibaba engineer summarized the appeal to Reuters as making one expensive query do repeated work.

The approach is especially attractive when access to advanced chips is constrained. Training a frontier model remains compute-intensive, but a company does not need to rediscover every behavior independently if it can buy or obtain high-quality demonstrations from an existing system. Distillation therefore changes the economics of the race. The leader pays for exploration; followers can sometimes pay less to learn from visible results.

## Industrial policy turns a technique into infrastructure

Reuters found that Chinese companies are building software to automate output collection and employing specialist workers to prepare the resulting material. Government policy is also encouraging the broader technical category. A national AI-and-manufacturing package issued by eight agencies in January supports training, fine-tuning, and distillation for industrial uses, while Shanghai and Shenzhen have promoted methods that reduce computing requirements.

The policies do not instruct companies to extract American models. They support distillation generally as a way to spread smaller, cheaper systems through manufacturing and other industries. That is an important boundary in the evidence. The same technique can be used legitimately with a company's own models, licensed teachers, open models, or public research data. Distillation itself is common worldwide and is not illegal.

The geopolitical dispute concerns how the training examples are obtained and used. OpenAI's terms restrict using its output to build competing models, subject to exceptions, and Anthropic imposes a similar restriction without permission. US officials told Reuters that organized collection at industrial scale could amount to intellectual-property theft. Chinese officials described distillation as a standard development method and said they were open to acting where conduct crossed into IP theft, but made no concrete commitment in previously unreported July talks in Chengdu.

That leaves a difficult enforcement line. A model provider must distinguish ordinary high-volume customers, benchmarking, research, data-generation services, and competitive extraction. Unlike a physical chip, model output can be copied and turned into training material, so providers may need to infer purpose from patterns of queries and downstream use rather than from the transfer of a clearly identifiable product.

## Chip controls can slow discovery without containing diffusion

A US official argued that industrial-scale distillation is the main reason Chinese models have narrowed the gap with American rivals. Two people familiar with Chinese developers disputed that claim, saying distillation was one factor alongside advances in algorithms, data, engineering, and model design. Reuters' evidence establishes widespread use; it does not isolate how much of any model's performance came from distillation or prove that the technique is the primary cause of competitive progress.

That uncertainty should narrow the policy conclusion. Distillation does not make chip controls irrelevant. Frontier training, large-scale experimentation, and high-volume inference still require compute, and the article explicitly says the technique cannot reproduce a leading model's full capabilities. But controls aimed only at hardware cannot prevent techniques already exposed through commercial model access from diffusing. Once a capability can be demonstrated repeatedly, it can become training material for systems running on less powerful infrastructure.

The dependence also runs both ways. The Shanghai Jiao Tong researchers warned that excessive distillation could suppress original innovation by making developers reliant on breakthroughs from stronger providers. A student trained to imitate today's visible answers may perform well on selected tasks while remaining weaker on unfamiliar problems. Distillation is a shortcut to behaviors that have already been elicited, not a substitute for discovering the next architecture, training method, or capability.

## What the documents establish

Reuters' review is stronger than a single vendor's abuse report because it draws on many public Chinese sources and identifies specific projects. Yet it is not an audit of the resulting model weights or a measurement of causal contribution. Alibaba, Meituan, Shanghai Jiao Tong University, China's Ministry of Industry and Information Technology, and the Cyberspace Administration of China did not respond to Reuters' questions. The article also does not show that every cited project violated provider terms, used deceptive access, or incorporated the examples into a commercial release.

The durable finding is narrower and more consequential: frontier-model output has become a strategic input. It can be collected, curated, and converted into cheaper local capability, while policy and industry turn that process into repeatable infrastructure. The competitive moat around a model therefore depends on more than chips and weights. It also depends on access rules, detection, contracts, the cost of generating useful demonstrations, and the harder-to-copy capacity to produce genuinely new ideas after imitation reaches its limit.
