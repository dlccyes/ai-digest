## Censorship with a double standard

In a country whose censors quickly suppress disfavored speech, the government's response is revealing. That bias reflects the outlook of the political system. China has never had a female national leader in modern times, and no woman has served on the Politburo Standing Committee. Officials nevertheless lecture women about becoming "virtuous wives and good mothers," as though declining marriage and birth rates were mainly matters of attitude.

## Make marriage fairer, not compulsory

The government cannot restore the women erased by past population policy, but it can make marriage a safer and more equitable choice. Enforcement against harassment, marital rape and domestic violence remains weak. Fairer divorce proceedings, reliable child-support payments and an easier path out of abusive marriages would give women more confidence that marriage will not trap them.

Those reforms could also help erode the system of bride prices. Economic policy matters too. The state could turn some of China's large stock of unsold homes into affordable rentals for young families, lowering one of the biggest barriers to marriage. Recent reforms to the *hukou* household-registration system are a useful step because they make it easier for migrants and their children to gain access to urban schools and public services.
