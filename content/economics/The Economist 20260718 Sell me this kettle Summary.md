## One kettle, many identities

## When positioning becomes costume

The joke works because none of these campaigns changes the product's central job. Each borrows a currently fashionable language - exclusivity, design, artificial intelligence, sustainability, generational identity, authenticity, wellness or political grievance - and stretches it until the mismatch becomes obvious. The names and slogans create the appearance of differentiation while the kettle underneath remains much the same.

That does not make brand positioning useless. Different customers care about different qualities, and a clear message can help a genuinely distinctive product find the people who value it. The satire instead targets the moment when the story outruns the evidence. Ordinary features become innovations, unnecessary complications become progress and vague cultural signals substitute for a reason to buy.

The practical Brewmaster pitch may be unglamorous, but it makes a promise a customer can test. The alternatives reveal a common temptation in marketing: when growth slows, companies may prefer inventing a grander identity to improving the object itself. A strong brand can give a product meaning. It cannot indefinitely conceal the distance between that meaning and what the product actually does.
